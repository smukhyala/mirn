A bench for one question

What does a method like yours read when the answer is nothing?

Everything on this page is simulated. The crowd is an invented model of pedestrians — invented people obeying invented rules — and no number here is a measurement of real pedestrians. What is real is the ruler: the same room is run twice, once with a robot and once without, from the same starting positions and the same random wobble, and the difference between a person's two paths is the robot's effect on them.

What this does

You have written a disturbance metric, or you are about to. Describe how it is computed and this bench will run the nearest thing it can run on a world where the robot's true effect on every person is exactly nothing — not nearly nothing, exactly — and tell you what a method of that shape reads there.

Nothing of yours is read in. No code, no file, no data. You answer five closed questions, those answers name one of a handful of families this bench already implements, and it runs its own estimator on its own rooms and reports its own numbers. What leaves this page is a claim about a shape of measurement, never a claim about your robot.

Five questions about how your number is computed

The link carries your five answers and nothing that was measured. There is no server behind this page, nothing is saved on this machine, and reloading re-runs the simulation on today's code rather than showing you an old answer.

Or hand it the metric itself

Write the body of a function. It is handed one run — the people's paths and the robot's path, which is what a corridor gives you — and returns a distance in metres. It is never handed the run without the robot: a metric that could see that could subtract it and return the exact answer, and what came back would describe this bench rather than your ruler.

It runs on this machine, in a worker with no page around it, on rooms where the robot's true effect on every person is exactly nothing. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved. What you type here never enters the link.

This can show that a metric is untrustworthy. It cannot show that one is sound — the crowd here is invented, so failing is a finding and passing is not a verdict.

The verdict

How every ruler this bench runs does on the same rooms

The described families are measured one after another, on the same rooms, at the same seeds, and against the same drift line. That is what makes the last column of the table a count of one thing rather than a heading over several. It takes a few seconds, and the line below says which ruler is being measured and how far along it is.

If you have already handed this page a metric of your own, it joins the table as your row. Nothing of yours is re-run from here and nothing of yours is needed: press this and the described rulers are measured whether or not you have written one.

What comes back is which shape of ruler this invented crowd confounded, and that is the whole of it. It is not a ranking of methods. There is no total, no share and no winner, and a ruler that made none of these mistakes here has been shown only that it made none of them here.
