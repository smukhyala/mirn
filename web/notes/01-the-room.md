A corridor, {{lit:22 m}} end to end. People are walking through it in both directions, the way
people walk through corridors. A robot comes in at one end and drives to the other. Everybody gets
where they were going.

Watch it before reading on.

```mirn:scene
id: first-crossing
preset: corridor-11
controls: [play, scrub]
showControl: false
caption: >
  One robot, one crowd, one crossing. Drag the scrubber to any moment. Nothing about this crossing
  is hidden from you.
```

That was one pass of the room, clock start to last person out, and it is the unit that everything
else here is built out of.

:::term{id=run}

Follow any one person with the scrubber and they leave a trail behind them. That trail has a name
too.

:::term{id=trajectory}

A trajectory is nothing more mysterious than a table: an x, a y, and a time, at every tick of the
clock, and this clock ticks every {{lit:0.05 s}}. Nothing in it is inferred and nothing is
smoothed. Every path on that canvas is recorded exactly, to the step.

## Try to answer it

So: how much did the robot change what those people did?

Scrub to the moment the robot passes closest to somebody. You can see it happen — the path bends
away from the robot, and then the robot is past and everyone carries on. Good. Now say how far
sideways. Not how far from the wall, not how far from the robot. How far from where that person
would have been.

There is no *would have been* on the canvas. There is one path. They walked it once, with a robot
in the room, and that is the whole of what happened.

Notice that this does not improve with equipment. Point a sharper camera at the corridor and you
get the same single path, more precisely. Track more people, record for longer, tick the clock
faster: all of it gives you more of exactly what you already have. The thing you are missing was
never in the room to be recorded.

You have a ruler and nothing to hold the other end of it against. The measuring was always going
to be the easy half.

:::caveat
The crowd here is a toy. Each person is a dot with a spot on the far wall to reach, steered there
by a short formula and shoved aside by anyone who comes close, and the room they are in was
invented this morning. The question asked of them is not a toy. It is the same question you would
ask of an afternoon of footage from a real building, and the footage would leave you stuck in
exactly this place.
:::

## Answer it anyway

The question again, then. How much did the robot change what these people did? Pick a number and
write it down before you move on.

You cannot check that number against anything, and neither can I. Everything after this page is a
response to that.
