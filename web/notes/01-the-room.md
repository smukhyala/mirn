A corridor, {{lit:22 m}} end to end, with people walking through it in both directions. A robot
comes in at one end and drives to the other. Everybody gets where they were going. Watch it before
reading on.

That was one pass of the room.

:::term{id=run}

Follow one person with the slider.

:::term{id=trajectory}

A trajectory is a table and nothing more mysterious: an x, a y, and a time at every tick of a
{{lit:0.05 s}} clock. Nothing is inferred, nothing smoothed.

## Try to answer it

So: how much did the robot change what those people did?

Scrub to the moment the robot passes closest to somebody. The path bends away, then everyone
carries on. Now say how far sideways — not from the wall, not from the robot, but from where that
person would have been.

There is no *would have been* on the canvas. There is one path, walked once, with a robot in the
room.

Equipment does not help. A sharper camera gives you the same single path, more precisely. The thing
you are missing was never in the room to be recorded, and you are left holding a ruler with nothing
to put the other end of it against.

Pick a number anyway and write it down. You cannot check it against anything, and
neither can I. Everything after this page is a response to that.

:::caveat
The crowd here is a toy: dots steered toward a spot on the far wall by a short formula and shoved
aside by whoever comes close, in a room invented this morning. The question asked of them is not a
toy. You would ask it of an afternoon of footage from a real building, and be stuck in exactly this
place.
:::
