A bench for one question

Does the invented crowd walk like this one?

Every other page here shows numbers about an invented crowd, and says so above them. This page is the exception, and it is the only one: it puts a real recording of real people on one side of a comparison. So it says three different things instead, and it says them before any number.

How every number here is worked out

What this does

Both crowds this bench runs were calibrated against nothing at all. Their parameters came from a demonstration and from being measured against each other, and that is the single largest thing standing between this bench and an instrument: it can show that a measurement is confounded, and it cannot say by how much, because the crowd it says that about was tuned to look plausible and fitted to no observation.

So: hand it a recording of people walking. It runs the invented crowd at each of a range of walking paces and wobbles, and reports how far the crowd's distribution of walking speeds sits from the recording's — and, for each of those two settings, whether your recording actually decides it or merely fails to rule anything out.

What it will never do is produce a disturbance number. That is arithmetic rather than caution. Every figure this site reports is a difference between two runs of one room, once with a robot and once without, from the same starting positions and the same wobble. A corridor cannot be filmed twice. There is no second recording in which the same people, on the same day, in the same mood, walked past no robot — so the quantity this whole site exists to measure is not in your file, and a number claiming otherwise would be the confound this project teaches against, wearing real data.

Your recording

A text file, one sample per line, four values separated by spaces or commas: the frame number, who it is, and where they were in metres across and metres up. Lines beginning with a hash are ignored, so a file with a header block reads as it stands. This is the shape the public pedestrian datasets are distributed in.

It is read in this browser and goes nowhere. There is no server here to send it to, nothing is saved, and it never enters a link. Who each person is, is used to group the samples and then thrown away before anything is measured — no name from your file can reach this page, and no single person's path is drawn or reported.

Frames are not seconds and nothing here can work out the difference for you. A file annotated at 2.5 frames per second and read as 25 describes a crowd walking at a tenth of its speed, and every fitted number after it is wrong by the same factor without anything looking wrong.

The rest of the bench

Nothing on this page is a disturbance number, and nothing that is one lives here. Those are on the console, where an invented crowd is run twice and the difference between a person's two paths is the robot's effect on them; the referee drill asks you to call one of those before the second run is shown; the method card measures what a metric of a given shape reads on a world where the answer is exactly nothing; and the working sets out the arithmetic for all of it.
