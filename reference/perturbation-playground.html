Counterfactual Crowd

Two worlds run side by side on the same random seed: one with a robot, one without. Because the counterfactual is simulated rather than estimated, the robot's true effect on the crowd is exactly knowable — and can be compared against what a real study would have measured. The gap between them is not physics. It is the forecaster's error wearing a causal label.

What you are looking at

Pedestrians follow a social-force model with a per-agent seeded noise stream. Both worlds draw from the same stream, so every difference between a solid path and its ghost is caused by the robot and nothing else. True is the mean paired displacement across the crowd.

Measured imitates what the literature actually computes: a constant-velocity forecast stands in for the counterfactual, and the residual against the observed path is reported as perturbation. Stretch the forecast horizon and that residual grows — even though the robot's real influence has not changed at all.

Turn People see robot off and true perturbation collapses to zero, because the humans are physically incapable of responding. Measured perturbation does not. Everything it still reports is phantom — and that is the failure mode this project exists to fix. The shaded band on the trace is the detection floor: any effect inside it is smaller than the instrument's own error, and cannot be resolved.

A toy social-force crowd is exactly the kind of environment that would make the research question circular, so this page is an intuition pump and a teaching device — not evidence. The real estimates come from paired conditions in PeRoI.
